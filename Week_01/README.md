# Week 01 — Numerical Errors and Floating-Point Representation

## Overview

The first week introduces some of the fundamental concepts required for numerical computation. The main focus is on understanding how numerical errors arise, how numbers are represented computationally, and how errors can propagate through numerical calculations.

These concepts provide the foundation for analyzing the accuracy and reliability of the numerical methods studied throughout the course.

## Topics

### 1. Introduction

General introduction to computational astrophysics and the role of numerical methods in solving mathematical problems arising in astrophysical applications.

### 2. Errors in Numerical Methods

- Numerical errors
- Iterative methods
- Significant figures

This section introduces the different sources of error that may appear in numerical computations and the importance of quantifying their effect on numerical results.

### 3. Binary and Floating-Point Representation

- Binary representation
- Floating-point representation
- IEEE 754 encoding

This section examines how real numbers are represented in computers and how finite-precision arithmetic can introduce rounding errors into numerical calculations.

### 4. Error Propagation and Taylor Series

- Error propagation
- Taylor series expansions
- Truncation error

Taylor expansions provide an important tool for constructing and analyzing numerical approximations. In particular, they allow us to characterize truncation errors and study how approximations behave as the discretization parameters are varied.
